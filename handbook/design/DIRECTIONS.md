# DFO Online Handbook: design directions

Three directions, each meant to feel professional, helpful, comprehensive, visually pleasing and beautifully designed. All three share the same rules: original artwork only, generous whitespace, light (vellum) and dark (ink) themes of equal quality, one brass accent family, one cool sea colour for links and maps, and ornament only where it helps a reader find their place. Body text and links meet WCAG 2.2 AA contrast in both themes. Brass is used for rules, numerals and marks, never for running text. Recommended: **A**, with **B** as the fallback if A reads too old-world on phones.

## Direction A: Field Atlas

A well-made printed atlas brought onto the screen. The page is warm vellum (`#F4EEE1`) with deep iron-gall ink text (`#1F2328`). Brass (`#8C6D2F` for rules and marks, a lighter `#B8944A` for ornaments in the dark theme) sets off a sea blue-green (`#2D6575`) for links and map lines. Headings are set in Cormorant Garamond (semibold, tight tracking) over Source Serif 4 body text at a comfortable 18 px with a 1.6 line height. Inter handles small interface labels: search, filters, the "Where are you?" selector. Each of the 11 stages gets a Roman numeral in a thin brass ring and a short hairline rule, like plate numbers in an atlas. The stage map is an abstract route of numbered waypoints joined by a dotted sea-lane, not a geographic map. Illustrations are engraved copperplate linework with one restrained colour wash, wide and quiet, with no text in them. The dark theme is ink (`#14171B`) with parchment-toned text (`#E9E1CF`), the same brass and a lifted sea (`#6FA9B6`). It should feel like the same book read by lamplight, not a different site.

## Direction B: Museum Guide

The modern museum handbook: calm, airy and placard-clear, with the medieval Bay felt only in the details. A neutral off-white (`#F7F5F0`) and soft charcoal (`#22252A`) carry IBM Plex Sans for body and interface, with IBM Plex Serif for headings and article titles. The grid is strict, with wide margins, and every "At a glance" box and callout looks like a gallery label: a small brass heraldic mark, a one-line title, short facts. Brass (`#8A6A2C`) appears only as thin keylines and stage numerals in plain Arabic figures. Links use a cool harbour blue (`#2B5F7E`). The stage map is a clean horizontal or vertical timeline of eleven tiles, each with a cropped square illustration, built for thumbs on a phone. Illustrations are flat gouache with soft grain, in a muted palette of slate, ochre, sea-green and dusk rose. The dark theme is graphite (`#16181B`) with soft white text, and the illustrations sit on a slightly lifted panel so they never glow. Best for skimming and for readers in short sessions on small screens.

## Direction C: Sea Chart

A navigator's chart for a game that is largely about crossing the Bay. The dark theme leads here (deep sea-ink `#0F1C24`, chart-paper text `#E6DFCB`), and the light theme is a pale chart paper (`#F2EEE3`) with ink-blue text (`#1B2A35`). Headings are Alegreya (bold, slightly condensed), body text is Alegreya Sans, and stage numerals are set like chart soundings. A very faint grid of rhumb lines sits behind the home page only. The stage map is a voyage: eleven numbered anchorages on an invented, schematic coastline that echoes no real game map, joined by a course line, with a small original compass mark as the only flourish. Brass (`#A8843C`) marks the compass, rules and the current stage. Sea teal (`#4C9CA3` dark, `#2A6A70` light) marks links. Illustrations are ink-wash with fine chart linework laid over them, so they belong on the chart. It is the most distinctive and memorable direction, and the riskiest for legibility, so the article pages drop the grid and chart ornament entirely and read as plainly as A.
