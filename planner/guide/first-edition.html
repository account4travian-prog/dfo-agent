01

Your three questions

1. "Dungeons are public and full of people"

That's how DFO's online world works. A named dungeon is one shared room for everyone: what another player kills stays dead for whoever comes next, and loose loot goes to whoever picks it up first. A cleared dungeon's foes and chests come back only after 20 real minutes (40 for an elite dungeon). So following a crowd into a popular dungeon means walking through rooms someone else already emptied.

There are two ways out:

  • Quest dungeons are private. When one of your quests names a dungeon (or a building), its door asks Public or Your instance. Your instance is a copy for you and your party only. It is also the dungeon at its full size: online, most public dungeons are built smaller. An instance remembers what you cleared, and respawns on a real-time clock like a public dungeon. Taking quests is how you get dungeons to yourself.
  • The wilderness is full of dungeons nobody has found. Online only, every empty map square (about 820 m across) has a 30% chance of a spawned dungeon and 10% of an Elite one. They never stand on a road or within 300 m of a town. Every player sees the same ones, but almost nobody walks off the roads to find them. Leave the road and they turn up all around you.

2. "Should I move away from the big gatherings?"

Yes, but step by step, and not into the mountains. Distance itself doesn't make foes stronger. What sets the danger is what kind of place you're in (BAL3):

Better places drop better loot: the same tier sets the odds of rare items. Read a dungeon's type before going in. Online the sky runs a day every real hour (midnight on the hour, dawn at quarter past), so night comes round often. Wander by day while you're weak.

Stay out of the Wrothgarian Mountains for now. Most of that region is the Open Zone: open PvP, foes 4x stronger, and dying there drops everything in your bags (and your cart), and a player who kills you takes one equipped piece. The loot is double, but it's a place for a geared group, not a new character.

3. "Are the professions worth it?"

Yes, as a support skill, not as your main source of power. The details are further down. In short:

  • Crafted gear tops out at Masterwork, which is about one Rare roll. The best gear (Legendary, Atheric sigil sets, artifacts) comes from bosses and hard content, not the anvil.
  • But for Tiger Fist, Alchemy is close to essential. You have no spells and no magicka, so potions are your only healing, cures, levitation and water breathing in the field.
  • Gathering is unlimited and earns gold on the global market. Gold buys power: skill swaps, specialisations, repairs and other players' crafts.
  • You can raise only two crafts above Journeyman (50), for good. Choose them deliberately (see below).
02

How power works online: three ladders

  1. Daggerfall level and skills. Your class has the fastest skill growth the game allows (x0.30, doc 05). Online, Master Skills is always on: up to five class skills can go past 100, to 200 (two primary, two major, one minor).
  2. Renown, online's own level, 1 to 50. Each level adds +3 health and +2 magicka online, and it pays for stars on the Firmament (a Path-of-Exile-style passive tree with 518 stars).
  3. Gear: loot rarity (Common → Magic → Rare → Legendary → Artifact), gem sockets, sigil sets, techniques.

Two lessons:

  • Crowds help if you join them. Grouping doesn't split XP: a party of four earns 130% of each kill each. If a dungeon is full of players, invite them to a party instead of competing with them.
  • Quests and raids pay best early. Quests also send you to private dungeons and pay gear (next section).

The hourly cap is 15,000 Renown XP per account, and a solo player never reaches it.

The game pushes back after level 10 / Renown 15

Since 2026-10-17 (BAL11, section 17), foes grow with you. For each Daggerfall level past 10 their blows land 7% harder and their health grows 4%, compounding, plus a smaller amount per Renown past 15. At level 20 / Renown 30 a foe hits 2.3x as hard as at level 10. You stay ahead only if your gear and Firmament keep up with your level. Don't rush levels on weak equipment.

04

A plan for Tiger Fist

Now (level 1–5, Renown 1–5)

  1. Bank your gold. A death online costs a tenth of the gold you carry (not the bank), and you rise at the nearest safe place at half health.
  2. Join the Fighters Guild (or any guild) and run its quests. Each one gives you a private dungeon and a gear choice. This beats queuing behind other players in public dungeons.
  3. Do The Court Crafter when its letter arrives (level 2) to start Herbalism and Alchemy (see below), and Written in the Stars (the Firmament) at level 3.
  4. Wear Gauntlets. Bare-handed techniques need them: Haymaker (x1.8, the strongest single hit in the game) and Flying Kick (hits everything where you land).
  5. Hunt the wilds by day for spawned dungeons. Mines and natural caves are the gentlest (tier 4).

Building up (level 5–10, Renown 5–15)

  1. Elite foes appear from Renown 5. Learn their look before you fight them alone.
  2. The Herald's Call (the Arena) and The Bay's Troubles (the watch) questlines at level 6. Its raids lead you to town defences, the biggest single Renown payout, and these are where other players are worth meeting.
  3. Try an Elite spawned dungeon (marked "Elite"): three foes per spawn point with double health and damage, but 20% better drops. Bring a partner.
  4. Spend Firmament stars on the fist line first. Since BAL10 (2026-10-17) every blow bonus finally reaches bare hands: the Firmament's fist stars (Iron Knuckles: fist damage, Strength, +4 Hand-to-Hand), sigil-set powers and loot powers. The Steed's sign holds the biggest fist channel (up to 28%).

Power (level 10+, Renown 15+)

  1. The Burning Doors (level 10) begins the Dagon Cycle: Dagon's Breaches (Oblivion Gate world bosses), then the Elite Breach. This is where Atheric gear and titles come from.
  2. The Hour That Was Not (level 12) opens the Super Dungeon, the hardest dungeon content.
  3. Revenants hunt from Renown 10. When a strong foe is about to flee, finish it, or it may come back stronger.
  4. Past 100: Hand-to-Hand keeps growing (its damage reads the skill, about x1.37 a blow at the top).
  5. Martial specialisation, a long-term goal. The 15 martial paths each need two weapon skills mastered, and Tiger Fist has only one (Hand-to-Hand). A skill swap (100,000 gold) can trade one unmastered class skill for a weapon skill in the same group. Swapping Medical (a major) for Blunt Weapon would open the Brawler path (Knockout, a heavy blow at x1.6), or swap in Short Blade for the Shadowdancer (+10 Agility, +15 Dodging). Choosing a path is permanent unless you pay 200,000 gold to reset it.

Your build's weak spots, and what covers them

  • Willpower 15: spells land about 60% of their damage on you and poison gets through most of the time. Carry Cure Poison and resist potions. Alchemy covers this.
  • No spells: no Recall, Levitate or Water Breathing. Use potions and, online, the Portal Stone for travel.
  • Personality 10: townsfolk and shopkeepers like you less, so expect worse prices in shops.
05

The professions, in detail

Ten professions: five that gather (Mining, Logging, Herbalism, Hunting, Fishing) and five crafts (Smithing, Building, Outfitting, Provisioning, Enchanting). Each runs from 0 to 100. Ranks are Novice, then Apprentice at 25, Journeyman at 50, Expert at 75 and Master at 100. Reaching rank n takes 10 × n² XP, so Master is 100,000.

  • Gathering has no limit. You can raise all five.
  • Only two crafts can pass 50, and that choice is permanent. A third craft stops just short of rank 51.
  • Specialisations unlock at 50 and 100 (changing one costs 1,000 Marks and a week).
  • Crafting quality depends on how far your rank is above the recipe's. Well above it (45+ ranks), you get Superior 52% of the time and Masterwork 8%. Superior carries a Magic roll and Masterwork a Rare roll plus your name on the item.

Recommended for Tiger Fist:

PickWhy
Provisioning (Alchemy + Cooking)potions are your healing and your utility. At 50, Brewer makes 3 potions a brew. Cooking feeds you under Climates & Calories.
Smithing (Armoursmith at 50)armour wears out, and you need repair kits and armour in the metals you're allowed (iron, silver, mithril, adamantium, ebony, orcish, daedric).
Herbalism + Miningfeed both crafts, and sell the surplus. With Deep Delver (Mining at 50), dungeon veins yield double.

Skip Enchanting and Jewelcrafting's spell lines (focus, cast speed, potency). They do nothing for a fighter with no magicka.

Quests and character growth

Questlines and private quest instances

What and how: quests now have objectives and branches in one Questlines window. Gear rewards offer a choice fitted to your skills. Guilds show their quest ladders. Online quest failure does not cost faction standing. A quest destination can offer a private instance for you and your party, at full dungeon size.

Where: follow the courier letters as your character levels. The guide lists the introductions from level 2 through level 12. Most require a registered account. See the guide's quests and crowded-dungeons sections for the rules and reward tiers. Private instances are included here as context for Questlines, not claimed as a new instance system in this interval.

Tiger Fist: guild quests are the direct route to private fights and useful gear.

Growth puts the progression screens together

What and how: the pause menu's Growth tab contains Level, Specializations, Firmament and Captain's Stars. The specialisation guide shows all thirty paths, their requirements, passives and signatures, and whether this character can choose them. The trees can open within the tab or full screen. This reorganises access; it does not grant free masteries.

Where: pause, then Growth. Captain's Stars applies where you have ships to captain. The classic interface also has routes into the guide.

Tiger Fist: use the guide to see which second weapon mastery would open a martial path.

Skill swaps and paid path resets

What and how: a skill swap costs 100,000 gold and replaces an unmastered class skill with a miscellaneous skill in the same group. It does not move the skills' values or grant levels. The latest fix prevents repeated swaps from recycling training into extra levels. Giving up an arcane or martial specialisation costs 200,000 gold per family; the masteries stay permanent. Online, coins, letters of credit and the Empire's bank account can pay.

Where: Growth, then Specializations. See the guide's long-term plan for Brawler and Shadowdancer.

Tiger Fist: this is how a one-weapon class can acquire a second mastery route. Budget for training as well as the swap.

Fairer online health growth and visible skill progress

What and how: online maximum health has a floor based on your class's average health roll and your current permanent Endurance, including Firmament Endurance. Raising it therefore helps earlier levels too. Better-than-average stored health is kept. Temporary spell and ring Endurance does not raise this floor. New online level-up attribute pools are fixed at 5 points; an already pending pool keeps its roll.

The enhanced Stats page now shows uses toward each skill point, when it is ready, and why it cannot advance. Ready skills still rise through rest or travel, at most once every 6 game hours; filling the bar alone does not award the point. The HUD also announces ready skills. This ticker is not drawn on the classic sheet.

Where: the health floor applies online; read skill progress on enhanced Stats.

Tiger Fist: later Endurance investment can repair a below-average health history. Check the skill ticker before assuming combat practice was wasted.

Combat, enemies and revenants

The hero's threat

What and how: ordinary dungeon and wilderness foes answer Daggerfall levels past 10 and Renown past 15. The increases compound. The level component stops growing at 30, the Renown component at 50. Tuned relay bosses use their own rules. See the guide's power section for the practical example.

Tiger Fist: keep gear and Firmament investment alongside levels.

Daggerfall Enemy Expansion

What and how: DEX 1.3.4 adds fifty enemies, changes classic enemy stats, and supplies dungeon and climate encounter tables, spells and on-hit effects. Dungeon table choices are seeded, so returning to the same dungeon does not reroll its table. With DEX loaded, enabling Meaner Monsters uses its compatible MUDEX tables. Changing these switches waits for the next game load. Online encounters follow the room's configuration.

One important rule: DEX trolls leave an unlootable body and rise again after 10 seconds of game time. Ordinary weapon hits and arrows do not destroy that body. A spell missile that reduces its corpse entity's health to zero ends the recovery. This is not a rule that any fire-looking weapon hit will work.

Where: ordinary encounters with DEX enabled, not a Renown quest unlock. It is on by default. Appropriate Morrowind replacements are used where supported; other enemies retain sprites.

Tiger Fist: fists alone cannot finish the recovering corpse. Leave it behind or bring a partner who can land the required spell missile.

The Unquiet: rarer rivals with escape tricks

What and how: only eligible special foes with the Unquiet mark can become new revenants. One in four eligible special foes receives it, and no new one rises while three hunt you. Escaping now drives their growth. From rank II, they use smoke, a blink, a sink into the ground or a faster run, according to their kind. Strike a sinking foe before it disappears to corner it; strike a blinking foe as it fades to interrupt it. Silence or paralysis can also prevent the blink.

Escapes leave wounds. At most three persist; the oldest heals after 5 game days left alone. An open-world escape can reveal its nearest reachable den. A warm trail lasts 12 character-clock hours; catching it there gives it 60% of its usual health. Personalities also determine vengeance, mastery or plunder ambitions. Mastery rivals can train against your habits.

Where: revenants open at Renown 10. Look for Unquiet in the special foe's title, then the journal's rival record and lair mark.

Tiger Fist: hand-to-hand leaves the cracked wound, multiplying poise by 0.7. Interrupt escapes with a hit; a warm trail is an opportunity to finish the fight.

The Dread and sworn companions' stars

What and how: a rank-V revenant that escapes again can become the Dread if it has a lair and no other Dread lives. It waits there permanently, stops hunting, has two last stands and a Legendary trophy. This is a late outcome of the rival system, not a separate level-unlock letter.

Once you have a sworn revenant, it has thirty purchasable stars around the Oath. Blade improves its body and blows, Grave its art, and Road its errands and loyalty. It earns levels through fights beside you, and errands below companion level 10; its cap is 20. Each level past the first and each original rank past the first supplies a point. Stars must connect to the Oath. Refunds are free while it is not abroad; removing a connecting star also removes stars that depended on it.

Where: Companions, then Stars for a sworn revenant; pursue the Dread at its lair.

Tiger Fist: a companion is another source of combat support. Choose combat stars for an active partner or Road stars for errands.

Elite Breach refinements

What and how: this interval improves an existing activity. Lords are sized for members who reached the hall's arrival plate; a late arrival adds its share without healing the lord's existing health fraction. Once a lord wakes, a member's death no longer removes that share. Surviving players must finish it.

Athenaeum stones and their lever handles are now visible and usable. Waiting on a timed crossing counts as standing there, and a door refusing passage explains why. Damage numbers show what the room accepted. Killing a Gauntlet Caster counts as silencing it. Solo Redoubt announcements identify a solo run. The Elite Breach also received its own score and interface work.

Where: existing Elite Breach runs, reached through the Dagon Cycle described in the guide.

Tiger Fist: deaths no longer make a waking lord easier. Keep the group alive.

The Witches' Festival

What and how: the 2026 online festival runs from 20 October through 7 November, UTC. Registered accounts earn account-held Soul Cakes through play and spend them at a Wyrd Sister beside a tavern door. Ordinary foes have a 25% chance to give one; undead and Daedra have a 50% chance. Elite finds give two. Gathering has a 20% chance; crafting and loot acts have a 30% chance. Daily finds cap at 120 cakes. The sky's Witches' Festival and Tales and Tallows days, and the real UTC day of 31 October, double finds; these do not multiply each other again.

The shop sells the Witchwarden title (60 cakes), Witchfire aura (150), three profile icons (40 each), six festival cards, and the bound Wyrd's Raiment set. Most set pieces cost 40, the cuirass 50 and Witchmoon Athame 60. Ordinary festival cards cost 20; King Lysandus' Spectre costs 45. Profile icons replace the portrait on account and Inspect displays.

A Hearth Offering bowl costs 5 cakes. Set it at an owned home's door, fill up to 10 cakes at a time, to a maximum of 60. Other accounts can take one per bowl per UTC day. Give away 25 for Hearthkeeper; take 10 from others for the Soul Cake icon. You can retrieve your bowl and its remaining cakes after the festival.

Where: online with a registered account during the calendar window; visit a tavern's Wyrd Sister. Offerings require your own home.

Tiger Fist: fighting and gathering earn the currency without magic. The Athame is Elven, which your class forbids; inspect each reward before buying it.

Starfall and The Thirteenth of Frostfall

What and how: during the festival, three stars fall each real hour near different towns in one coven's region: at :45, :52:30 and :00. Each has a 90-second omen and a 15-minute deadline after landing. Defend its light through four waves and a master. An empty site pauses its encounter clock, but the wall-clock deadline still arrives.

A held star pays each eligible defender their own spoils: gold based on 60 per character level, varied by 20% either way; 1–3 Starfall Glass; a Magic-or-better piece; and a 25% chance of a Starspun Weave piece. Registered-account rewards include three quests' worth of Renown and 30 silver, subject to the relevant caps; at most nine star claims count per UTC day. Participation matters: standing nearby alone is not enough. The Heart-Star on the sky's Witches' Night doubles gold and glass and guarantees the set piece.

The seasonal questline opens at level 6 for a registered online account. Meet a Wyrd Sister or hold a star to receive its lead. Morwen's story takes you to Glenmoril and the Coven on the Bluff and back to more stars. Begun lines remain visible out of season, but star steps wait for the event. The Starwarden title is earned through this line, not bought.

Where: follow the omen and map marks during the festival. The event and the level-6 questline are separate: the questline's level gate is not stated as an entry gate to the world encounter.

Tiger Fist: this offers group combat rewards without spells. Starspun gear is Elven, so your forbidden-material rule matters; the other rewards still have value.

The world, weather and travel

The Jungle of Bantha

What and how: the final jungle area covers Hammerfell's southeastern Bay shore, including Lainlyn, Satakalaam and Mournoth and neighbouring lands. The earlier north-of-Daggerfall location was superseded. It has layered trees, dense floor plants, humid haze and dirt trails. The later spacing pass opened more room between trunks.

Where: travel to the southeastern shore. In wood-bearing jungle map squares, Logging gets 48 tree slots per UTC day, four times the richest ordinary woods. This is not a guarantee of high-tier wood: unconfirmed jungle squares can supply Pine instead.

Tiger Fist: a rich Logging destination if you want sale materials; not a reason to abandon the recommended Herbalism and Mining support skills.

Rain, snow and ground cover follow the local air

What and how: on the enhanced rendering lane, precipitation depends on climate, season, time of day, height and drifting weather. Air blends across climate borders instead of switching abruptly at a map-square edge. Ground snow tracks what fell and has not melted. Peaks can be white while valleys are clear, and a winter jungle need not snow. Trees, ground, footsteps and grass read local cover.

Where: outdoors on the enhanced lane. The distant terrain ring still lacks this snow cover; do not read a white-free horizon as a promise of bare ground.

Tiger Fist: check local conditions when travelling. This is a weather presentation change, not evidence of a new damage bonus or penalty.

The Overworld has quest marks, forecasts and better routes

What and how: quest destinations are marked; the followed quest stays at the edge when off screen. Pressing a mark plans a road journey. Forecasts read weather for the time you are expected to reach each stretch, at that stretch's pace, rather than reporting only today's weather there. Dungeon models keep a fixed world size. Chasers remain visible as far as their band is drawn.

Road journeys follow the lane continuously. Town journeys use street routes at x5 speed, pass through open gates and stop with an explanation at gates shut for the night.

Where: use the Overworld travel view and its place cards.

Tiger Fist: quest routing and forecasts help you choose a journey before setting off.

Buy passage on a friendly ship

What and how: Activate a friendly ship moored at a port. Choose a known port from the captain's list, nearest first, and accept its quoted fare. The ship actually sails there through the Overworld crossing. This is a passage, not ownership. Stepping off ends it and brings anything in its hold ashore. An interrupted voyage can resume while you remain aboard.

The fare uses Daggerfall's ship-travel cost, counting the whole voyage as sea: 25 gold per 24 ocean map pixels, plus 25. The list gives the exact charge. Come Sail Away must be enabled; this does not take you beyond the Veil.

Where: the Bay's ports, beside a suitable friendly ship; no separate level or Renown gate is stated by the passage law.

Tiger Fist: another travel option without Recall or owning a vessel.

Solid props block your movement

What and how: trees block at their trunks; rocks, barrels, statues and similar solid props block at their feet. The player slides around them on foot or mounted. Grass and ferns remain passable. This does not add the same collision to enemies, arrows or the camera.

Where: solid world props, indoors and out.

Tiger Fist: leave room to circle obstacles during a fight; a trunk is not newly guaranteed protection from a missile.

Economy, trade and crafting

Each town has an economy

What and how: online towns have Prosperity from 0 to 100. Size, real-day market cycles, a seat's Standing and Edict, raids and player activity affect it. Bands run from Destitute through Struggling, Steady and Thriving to Booming. A raid subtracts 15 while it runs. A port's sea lanes add 2 each, up to three lanes.

Town prosperity alone moves asking prices from x1.25 at 0 to x0.75 at 100. Sale prices fall away from the middle in either direction; a booming town does not automatically pay more for your loot. Seat discounts can affect your asking price further, with a combined floor of x0.70. Repairs also read the town factor. The port exchange uses the town's buying and selling factors too.

Witnessed town activity, player-trader sales, successful defences and sea dealings contribute to the players' share. It is bounded at 30 Prosperity, with older activity fading by x0.8 per real day over fourteen days. Ordinary gold spending is not a direct donation to Prosperity.

Where: hover a town on the map for its economy and control card; see the effect at counters and port exchanges.

Tiger Fist: compare repair and supply prices between towns. Fighting a town raid helps its economy as well as your Renown.

High-metal death drops arrive more gradually

What and how: with Unleveled Loot active, an Orcish or Daedric bonus drop the mod already granted gets a second check below character level 20. It keeps its top metal with probability level / 20. Otherwise it becomes Elven at levels 1–4, Dwarven at 5–8, Mithril at 9–12, Adamantium at 13–16 or Ebony at 17–19. At 20 the extra ramp disappears. This changes that bonus drop's metal, not the chance of receiving a bonus drop in the first place. Material-replacement settings still apply.

Where: the mod's eligible Orc and Daedra death drops.

Tiger Fist: some early fallback metals are forbidden to you. Read the material before equipping; this does not weaken your fists.

Repairing found loot gives limited resale credit

What and how: a found piece keeps the condition it had when found. Online sale valuation credits that condition plus one third of condition restored above it by your own repair kits or repair effects, rounded down. Paying a smith adds no resale credit. Credit never exceeds the repair still present on the item.

Where: found gear sold online, including the relevant trader and patron sale paths.

Tiger Fist: repair equipment to use it. Paying a smith before selling it is not a way to raise its sale valuation.

Batch salvage and clearer crafting odds

What and how: the enhanced inventory's Batch salvage lets you tick pieces or select eligible Magic, Rare or junk pieces, then confirm once. Automatic selection excludes Legendary gear. Worn, locked, bound, quest, artifact, Aetheric and socketed pieces are among the refusals; remove gems first where appropriate. Each selected piece is checked again before breaking, so a newly refused piece stays.

Crafting stations now state their actual quality odds and say when a craft limit prevents experience. Home stations serve the owner's account. These interface improvements do not increase the two-craft limit or change quality into a guaranteed Masterwork.

Where: enhanced inventory and crafting station windows. See the guide for profession ranks, specialisations and the recommended crafts.

Tiger Fist: quicker cleanup between trips, and a clearer warning before wasting materials on training a capped craft.

Gathering nodes catch the light

What and how: harvestable pictures now glint on their own surface, with occasional sheen, rather than using the briefly introduced outline ring. The effect respects occlusion, fog and local light. The node you are currently working stays quiet so the gathering action's own cue remains readable. Nodes without a suitable picture retain a glow.

Where: nearby gathering nodes; the jungle's increased tree supply is described above.

Tiger Fist: helps locate herbs and ore without changing harvest yields.

Looks, sound and interface

Layered Gearing shows worn armour

What and how: Layered Gearing is the default on-foot sprite look, using the character's own male or female body until changed. Head, chest and leg slots drive its appearance, with leather, chain and plate families and plate metal colours. Gloves, boots and pauldrons do not independently change the picture. Silver, Elven and Dwarven appear as steel. Cloaks add their dyed hood and cape.

Your Layered Gearing view also shows other on-foot sprite players in their worn armour, even if they chose a different look. Mounted players, beasts and Morrowind bodies have their own display paths. This feature is still labelled WIP.

Where: account profile, Skin, Look: Skins or Layered Gearing (WIP). Choose Female or Male there if desired.

Tiger Fist: visible equipment is clearer, but this is a layered sprite body, not a promise of a fully race-specific Khajiit model.

Morrowind bodies move and carry weapons differently

What and how: the Body Motion feature adds third-person gait, weapon-specific carrying and whole-body melee motion over the Morrowind clips. Legs keep moving under a strike. Later passes steady held weapons, adjust dual-wield running and ease the legs. Two-handers are carried in both hands at a run. This changes presentation, not the damage rules.

Where: supported Morrowind bodies with the Body Motion feature enabled. It needs that optional asset/rendering route; it does not replace all sprite animation.

Tiger Fist: relevant only if using that body route.

A belt lantern and separate monster volume

What and how: the lantern slot in Enhanced Plus and DFO Grimoire marks one lantern for your belt. Lit there, it takes no hand. Lighting a different light extinguishes it but leaves it on the belt. The Morrowind waist lantern was also reduced to belt scale.

Audio now has Monster Volume from 0–100% for monster calls, attack sounds and pain cries. Weapon impact and parry sounds remain on Sound Volume; master volume still applies.

Where: the inventory's lantern slot and the Audio settings respectively.

Tiger Fist: belt light leaves your hands free, and monster calls can be turned down separately.

Clearer windows, hotbar and language

What and how: the enhanced hotbar gains a Diamonds style and a hover-and-key assignment route. The broader text passes replace opaque descriptions with direct instructions. Guild vaults gain item previews, search and rarity filters; guild Stores put their action form first, with empty Units meaning all that fits. Quest people now use the names written in their quests.

You can also send a private bug, exploit or account-security report through /bug, or the mailbox at /mail. The form says what build and app context it includes. Your reports show whether they have been read and how they were closed. A half-written draft survives closing and reopening the window in the same sitting.

Where: enhanced hotbar options, guild storage and the game's help and status text. These are presentation and usability changes; they do not grant a new combat bonus. The separate proximity-voice PR was reverted in this interval, so it is not listed as a shipped addition.

Fixes worth knowing

  • Shared outdoor bodies, building cupboards and dungeon search finds use a shared list with individually judged takes. Opening a stale “Empty” view should no longer wipe someone else's loot. Shop-shelf stealing races remain outside that fix.
  • Revenants no longer take letters of credit, horses, carts or deeds. Open Zone companion bags do drop, and companions cannot be sent on errands from inside it.
  • Fallen-through dungeon bodies return to the entrance. Lifts, late rocks and home furniture have fixes to avoid enclosing the player. The reported steep Daggerfall hill remains open, not claimed fixed.
  • Revenant lairs place their rivals on reachable ground; won ship raids can end; Hollows no longer select townless sea regions.
  • Questline letters are paced by arrival. Quest NPCs avoid standing inside residents. Town-map Find me and marked-building visibility have fixes.
  • Fish can cook from their bag, joinery benches appear at valid doors, and batch deposits use one save. Boats return their oars after a journey; cabins refuse entry with enemies near.
  • Damage-over-time and disintegration reach relay-held bosses; Vampiric Effect heals from damage actually dealt. A duel's bar shows its duel vitality. Incubating disease has an unnamed status tile.
  • The Hearth Offering interaction crash, Oblivion gate audio distortion and several travel, loading and inventory hitches were addressed. This overview does not claim a measured frame-rate improvement.
