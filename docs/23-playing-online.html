Your catch-up strategy

You can't out-grind someone with ten times your hours. You don't need to. DFO caps much of what one account can earn each hour, day and week, and wherever a cap exists, a hardcore player's extra hours stop counting. Three rules follow:

  1. Spend your time on capped rewards first. Take every daily allowance, every day. They reset at 00:00 UTC, and a 10-hour player gets exactly the same amount. The full list is in Caps, Timers and Catch-Up.
  2. Play the scheduled events, not the grind. Event rewards dwarf kills. One fallen star or one town defence pays Renown equal to 15–30 kills of level-8 foes (table below).
  3. Know the rules they learned by trial and error. This guide and its companion guides come from the game's code. Most players never see the exact numbers: the quest-letter rules, private instances, spawned dungeons off the roads, danger by place, the permanent crafting limit. Make no permanent mistake: those cost a hardcore player nothing to redo, but cost you weeks.

The world, region by region, is in The World, Region by Region; city control and guild politics are in City Control and Guilds.

Questions a New Player Asks

Short answers to the questions a newer DFO Online player usually has, for Tiger Fist (Khajiit, the custom hand-to-hand class, no magic, level 7, about Renown 5). Each answer links to where the guide explains it in full. Studied at DFO Online commit 4bba28c6c (2026-10-20).

Getting started

What should I do in my next session?

  1. Stand in a city with a General Store, outdoors, by day, so questline letters can arrive and a stalled step (like The Court Crafter's) can begin. Gothway Garden, next to Privateer's Hold, has one.
  2. Join the Fighters Guild (Gothway Garden has a hall) and take its quests: each gives you a private dungeon and a choice of gear.
  3. Buy your first 8 stars in the Firmament (refunds are free until Renown 10).
  4. Bank your gold before you go out: dying costs a tenth of what you carry.
  5. Check the event clock and catch a falling star or a raid if one is near.

See How to catch up and Building Tiger Fist.

Do I need a registered account?

Yes. A guest account can play, but most of the new questlines (The Court Crafter among them) only begin for a registered account, and the account service that runs professions, Festival Soul Cakes and Starfall claims refuses a guest. Other Players and Staying Out of Trouble lists exactly what a guest misses.

Why haven't my questline letters arrived?

Three rules hold them back: one new line starts per hour of your character's clock, a courier only hands a letter over in a town outdoors by day, and a step that needs a town building (a General Store, tavern, armorer or temple) can't start in the wilds or a hamlet without one. It retries about two real hours of play later. See Questlines.

What's the difference between my level and my Renown?

Your level is Daggerfall's: it rises as your skills rise and sets your health and your foes' level. Renown is online's own level, 1 to 50: it adds +3 health and +2 magicka a level online and pays 2 Firmament stars a level. Events, quests and kills earn it. See How power works online.

Getting stronger

What do I spend level-up points on?

Speed first (it shortens your swings), then Strength, then Luck and Agility, Endurance last. You get 5 points a level online. See Building Your Character.

Which stars do I buy?

Start at The Steed. Your first 8: Path of the Sign, Path of the Sign, Gate of the Sign, Starway, Starway (the only bridge to the fist stars), then three Endurance and Health stars. At Renown 10 you reach Iron Knuckles and Pankration and the +30% damage cap; after that, the plan spends on defence. Skip Steed's Charge. See The Star Tree (Firmament).

How do I use my special moves?

You need a pair of Gauntlets with a technique line (Haymaker or Flying Kick). Press the Weapon technique key, the mouse's back side button by default. See Your gauntlet techniques.

Which skills should I master at 100?

Hand-to-Hand first, then Critical Strike (or Dodging for safety), Running, Backstabbing, and later Blunt Weapon for the Brawler path. Masteries are permanent. See Building Your Character.

What gear should I look for?

The best armour in metals you may wear (not Steel, Elven or Dwarven), Gauntlets with a technique line, and lines that raise Hand-to-Hand, Speed, Strength, Luck or Agility. Weapon-only lines (weapon damage, leech, elemental, slayer) do nothing for fists. The dream piece is Gauntlets of the Rose. See Building Your Character.

Are professions worth it?

As support, yes: Alchemy keeps a no-magic fighter alive, and Smithing keeps your armour repaired. You may take only two crafts past rank 50, for good. See The professions.

Catching up with hardcore players

How can I keep up with people who play all day?

Take every capped reward every day (they reset at 00:00 UTC, so a 10-hour player gets no more than you), play the scheduled events instead of grinding, and avoid permanent mistakes. See How to catch up and Caps, Timers and Catch-Up.

When are the big events?

Festival stars fall at :45, :52:30 and :00 each real hour until 7 November; a Dagon's Breach opens every two real hours; raids roll every game day; the sea serpent rises every four real hours. One star or raid is worth 15–30 kills of Renown. See The event clock.

Should I party with strangers?

Yes. Kills aren't split: everyone earns the whole kill plus 10% per extra member, and mentor mode scales stronger players down to your level automatically. See Group up with the strong players.

Dungeons, the world and travel

Why are dungeons full of people, or already empty?

A public dungeon is one shared room: what others kill stays dead for 20 minutes. Take quests for private instances, or leave the roads to find spawned dungeons nobody has touched. See Dungeons without the crowds.

Where is it safe to go?

Danger comes from the type of place, not the distance from town: towns, mines and caves are gentlest; the wilds are harder at night; Crypts and up hit much harder. Stay out of the Wrothgarian Mountains (the Open Zone) for now. See Where it's safe and The World, Region by Region.

How do I find a town with a carriage, port, guild or General Store?

Use the map's Find a service filter and its Nearest to helper. Only cities have carriages: the nearest to Gothway Garden is Chesterwark, about 8 km south-west.

How do I travel fast without magic?

The hired carriage outside a walled city's gate (about 1 gold a travel day), Portal Stones (5 Welkynd Shards each), ship passage between ports, and party travel to your leader. See Getting Around.

Your companion

What is my companion, and what do I do with it?

A revenant you beat and chose to Spare. Keep it beside you, read its Art, aim for 90 loyalty, and spend its stars on health, then damage, then its Art. Missions are a simple timed reward for a second companion. See Companions.

Fighting

How do I fight well with my fists?

Watch for the enemy's glint and the shape marked on the ground, step out of it, and punish the foe while it's Open after a missed attack: an overreached foe takes 30% more damage, and a perfect dodge (inside the mark until the last quarter second, then out) keeps it open longer. There is no block button for you; moving is your defence. See Fighting Well.

Does the direction of my punch matter?

Yes. The default "Click or Hold" mode picks a random direction each click. A down swing does +4 damage at −10 to hit; up does −4 damage at +10 to hit; left and right are even. Switch to Gesture in Controls if you want to choose by dragging. See Fighting Well.

What kills new fist fighters?

Archers and casters you can't reach, poison (your low Willpower resists it poorly), and running out of fatigue: exhaustion near enemies costs 10% of your health each time. Close on ranged foes through cover, carry Cure Poison, and keep a Restore Fatigue potion on the hotbar. See Fighting Well.

What do I do when a fight turns bad?

Drink from the hotbar early, then sprint away and break line of sight. You can't rest beside enemies. If you die, you rise at a safe place and lose a tenth of the gold you carry, so bank it first. See Fighting Well.

Do I fight differently in a group?

Enemies pick their own targets and there's no taunt, so call a target and focus it together. Your swing can hit more than one foe in reach, so pull enemies away from allies before swinging into a crowd. See Fighting Well.

Money and loot

How do I make money early?

Sell dungeon loot at shops (Mercantile matters most, Personality a little), take bounties (about 30–50 gold plus a gear piece at your level), finish quests, and turn daily Marks into gold at the Empire's bank (up to 300 Marks a day at 8 gold each). See Money and Loot.

What do I do with loot?

Keep what you can use or list; sell common spares; salvage unwanted Magic-or-better pieces for Welkynd Shards (1 from Magic, 3 from Rare, 8 from Legendary), which buy Portal Stones (5 shards) and reforges. Don't pay a smith to repair something before selling it: it adds no resale value. See Money and Loot.

Where should I keep my money?

In the Empire's bank: online it's one account you can use at any bank counter, and dying never touches it. A letter of credit carries gold without weight (1% fee, at least 100 gold). Online loans are small: 1,000 gold per level. See Money and Loot.

What are Marks, shards and the other currencies for?

Marks (the game also says "silver") are the account's currency for the market, guild seats and star refunds; Welkynd Shards come from salvage and pay for Portal Stones and reforging; Soul Cakes are the festival's; Weir Keys open Elite Gates. See Money and Loot.

Should I buy a home?

Later. Online homes cost 5,000 to 250,000 gold depending on size and town, and give you a private cupboard, a bed and a door you can open to your party or guild. See Money and Loot.

Other players and trouble

Can other players attack me?

Not in ordinary play. PvP happens only in duels you accept (refereed on a separate bar, your real health is untouched), Arena bouts, guild sieges, and the Open Zone in the Wrothgarian Mountains. Loose loot in shared dungeons is first come, first served. See Other Players and Staying Out of Trouble.

How do I find a group or a guild?

Use chat: /lfg to look for a group, /world, /region and /trade channels, and invite people from their Inspect card or the F menu (parties hold eight). Guilds need a registered account. See Other Players and Staying Out of Trouble.

How do I stay out of trouble with the guards?

Don't take from shop shelves or strangers' cupboards, don't trespass or assault anyone. If the watch stops you, pay the fine or face the court: running or dying only ends the chase, it doesn't clear the crime. Bounty contracts on a region's board improve your standing there. See Other Players and Staying Out of Trouble.

How do I rise in the Fighters Guild?

Promotion checks reputation and skills: one guild skill must reach the next rank's high bar and two its low bar, and Hand-to-Hand counts. Online there's no waiting period once you qualify. See Other Players and Staying Out of Trouble.

Should I become a werewolf or a vampire?

Your disease immunity doesn't stop either curse. Lycanthropy's beast form gets +30 to skills including Hand-to-Hand, but only in beast form, with a monthly kill requirement. Vampirism's spells don't suit a no-magic build. Neither is a free bonus. See Other Players and Staying Out of Trouble.

What is city control, and should I care?

Player guilds compete for town seats each week (Sunday to Sunday, 18:00 UTC). A seat gives its guild shop discounts, a tithe and a hall. You can help a guild by walking its pledged town, fighting gates and delivering your own materials. See City Control and Guilds.

Group up with the strong players

  • Mentor mode is automatic. In a party, anyone three or more levels above the group is scaled down to it, so a veteran friend fights at your level and the dungeon spawns for it. You can share content with anyone.
  • Kills aren't split. Every member earns the whole kill, +10% per extra member in the room. Join a guild, party with its regulars, and let their knowledge carry you through events.

Where hours still win (be honest about it)

No daily cap stops a long session's character levels and skill practice, profession harvesting (until the day's map nodes run out), quest chains, repeated dungeon respawns, or market trading. You won't match a hardcore player there, and you don't need to: stay on par in capped rewards and knowledge, and spend your remaining time on one of these, not all of them.

Dungeons without the crowds

Public dungeons are often full of other players, and that's by design. A named dungeon is one shared room for everyone: what another player kills stays dead for whoever comes next, and loose loot goes to whoever picks it up first. A cleared dungeon's foes and chests come back only after 20 real minutes. So following a crowd into a popular dungeon means walking through rooms someone else already emptied.

There are two ways out:

  • Quest dungeons are private. When one of your quests names a dungeon (or a building), its door asks Public or Your instance. Your instance is a copy for you and your party only. It is also the dungeon at its full size: online, most public dungeons are built smaller. An instance remembers what you cleared, and respawns on a real-time clock like a public dungeon. Taking quests is how you get dungeons to yourself.
  • The wilderness is full of dungeons nobody has found. Online only, every empty map square (about 820 m across) has a 30% chance of a spawned dungeon and 10% of an Elite one. They never stand on a road or within 300 m of a town. Every player sees the same ones, but almost nobody walks off the roads to find them. Leave the road and they turn up all around you.

Now (level 1–5, Renown 1–5)

Your gauntlet techniques

Techniques are DFO's special moves, one key per weapon. Bare hands have two: Haymaker and Flying Kick.

You need the right gauntlets, not just any gauntlets. A technique is a rolled line on a specific pair of Gauntlets (any material), shown on the item card as e.g. Haymaker +18% [15-30]. You get it by finding one: each Magic piece has a 12% chance of a technique line, each Rare 30%, each Legendary 45% (never on a Common piece or a sigil-set piece). Look for it on loot, shop stock and quest reward cards. Wear them and fight bare-handed.

How to use it:

  • The key is "Weapon technique", by default the mouse's back side button (Mouse3). Keyboard players: rebind it in Controls → Combat (F2 and F4 are free). On a phone it's the Tech hold button; on a gamepad, a d-pad choice.
  • Haymaker goes on the press. Flying Kick aims while you hold the key (a marker shows where you'll land) and leaps on release; a quick tap kicks at whatever you're looking at.
  • It's a real blow through the normal combat formula: it can miss, and it uses your Hand-to-Hand, crits and every bonus you have. It won't fire if it would leave you with no fatigue, so watch your fatigue bar.
  • The HUD shows the technique's name and its cooldown, or the key when it's ready.
  • Techniques never hit another player, and they sleep in duels.

Using them well: open a fight with a Flying Kick into a group, then Haymaker the biggest threat every 9 seconds between normal punches. Athleticism (your class advantage) helps keep the fatigue for both.

Improving the line: at a Reforge, a technique can be turned into the other bare-hand one (Haymaker ↔ Flying Kick), and a Hone raises its value to the top of its band (Magic 5–15%, Rare 15–30%, Legendary 30–50%). A non-Exalted Legendary's technique can't be changed.

No technique gauntlets yet? Later, a martial specialisation (see the Power stage) gives a signature technique on the same key whenever your gauntlets carry no technique line: for example the Brawler's Knockout (x1.6) or the Strider's flying kick (x1.2).

Your build's weak spots, and what covers them

  • Willpower 15: spells land about 60% of their damage on you and poison gets through most of the time. Carry Cure Poison and resist potions. Alchemy covers this.
  • No spells: no Recall, Levitate or Water Breathing. Use potions and, online, the Portal Stone for travel.

The professions, in detail

Are professions worth it? Yes, as a support skill, not as your main source of power:

  • Crafted gear tops out at Masterwork, which is about one Rare roll. The best gear (Legendary, Atheric sigil sets, artifacts) comes from bosses and hard content, not the anvil.
  • But for Tiger Fist, Alchemy is close to essential. You have no spells and no magicka, so potions are your only healing, cures, levitation and water breathing in the field.
  • Gathering is unlimited and earns gold on the global market. Gold buys power: skill swaps, specialisations, repairs and other players' crafts.
  • You can raise only two crafts above Journeyman (50), for good. Choose them deliberately (see below).

Ten professions: five that gather (Mining, Logging, Herbalism, Hunting, Fishing) and five crafts (Smithing, Building, Outfitting, Provisioning, Enchanting). Each runs from 0 to 100. Ranks are Novice, then Apprentice at 25, Journeyman at 50, Expert at 75 and Master at 100. Reaching rank n takes 10 × n² XP, so Master is 100,000.

  • Gathering has no limit. You can raise all five.
  • Only two crafts can pass 50, and that choice is permanent. A third craft stops just short of rank 51.
  • Specialisations unlock at 50 and 100 (changing one costs 1,000 Marks and a week).
  • Crafting quality depends on how far your rank is above the recipe's. Well above it (45+ ranks), you get Superior 52% of the time and Masterwork 8%. Superior carries a Magic roll and Masterwork a Rare roll plus your name on the item.

1. Character level and attributes

Online uses classic skill leveling

The Oblivion Remaster-like “Virtue” system is present for offline play, but online is pinned to classic leveling. Skill raises are tallied from actions, then checked after more than 360 classic game minutes have elapsed. A raise adds to the level sum built from primary and major skills (with the lowest major removed) and the highest minor. The level formula is floor((current sum - starting sum + 28) / 15). The character sheet applies one level each time it handles a level-up; when the sum remains ahead, another check can make a further level available.

Each online level gives 5 attribute points. The class’s hit points per level are rolled normally, but online maximum health is floored at the average the class would have earned at the character’s current permanent Endurance:

floor(25 + h + (level − 1) × max(1, (trunc(h/2) + h)/2 + floor(END/10) − 5))

Here h is class HP per level. Tiger Fist has h = 30; if Endurance is still 70 at level 7, the floor is 202 HP. The live floor uses permanent Endurance, including Firmament gains, not temporary bonuses, and never lowers a higher rolled maximum. Renown’s separate health bonus is covered in The Play Guide.

For Tiger Fist: spend level points on Speed first, then Strength, followed by Luck and Agility, and Endurance after those. The Strongest Melee Build’s combat measurements found Speed gave the largest fist DPS improvement because it shortens swings; Strength increases fist damage; Luck improves criticals; Agility helps hit chance, especially early. INT, WIL and PER do not add melee damage. The health floor lets you defer Endurance without permanently losing the average HP for your current Endurance. Raise Endurance if you want the floor itself higher.

Skills’ 100 soft cap

Online Master Skills is always on. Unmastered skills can reach 100; a mastered class skill can reach 200. Past 100, the use cost is multiplied by 4x from 100–124, 8x from 125–149, and 16x from 150–199. Each over-100 point is worth one quarter of a normal point in formulas, with extra effective-skill milestones of +2 at 125, +3 at 150, +4 at 175, and +6 at 200. Thus 200 reads as effective 140, not 200, for formulas. The permanent skill screen still shows the real value.

For Tiger Fist: keep levelling at a pace your gear can support. Foes in dangerous dungeons scale as combat skills master and continue past 100. Do not treat the 200 cap as a free multiplier.

2. Raising skills in play

Before 100, a skill’s uses are banked in its use counter. After the 360-minute check, the game compares the counter with the skill’s next-raise cost. The base cost grows with the current skill value, the skill’s advancement multiplier, the career multiplier, and 1.04^level; Reflexes adjusts counted uses. The class’s difficulty multiplier is 0.30, the fastest class growth multiplier. Tiger Fist’s class and biography therefore already give very fast growth; keep using the skills you want to improve rather than grinding a low-value action.

Rest and time-advancing travel release eligible skill raises. The Stats screen reports use progress and the HUD can show when resting would raise a skill. The six-game-hour gate means the skill may have enough uses before a rest, but it is not awarded until the check is due.

6. Gear for a fist build

Armour, gauntlets and fatigue

Wear the best allowed-material armour you can maintain. Tiger Fist forbids Steel, Elven and Dwarven equipment; the other metals remain usable. PCAAO makes armour reduce damage after a hit rather than lowering the chance to be hit. The struck body part and armour material determine the reduction. In the hit formula, the target’s Dodging skill is halved and subtracted from the attacker’s chance; the code does not apply an armour-weight penalty to Dodging. A normal swing that reaches its hit frame costs 8 fatigue (11 base × 0.75 drain scale, truncated), whether it hits or misses; armour and gauntlet material do not change that. Swing time reads Speed, weapon heft and weapon handling. Bare fists have no weapon weight and their handling is 0.9. Armour weight is not in the swing formula. Armour condition can affect armour performance when that module is enabled.

Gauntlets are useful for technique access and may carry compatible gear affixes, sigils and sockets as gloves. Their metal does not change the fist’s weapon damage, and a fist does not fail an enemy’s weapon-material threshold. Gauntlet affixes and enchantments can still improve the wearer: for example, Gauntlets of the Rose carry +14 armor, +12 Strength and +25 Hand-to-Hand, plus an armor enchantment, alongside Open Hand. BAL10 made the blow modifiers apply in both combat cores to unarmed hits. Put on any Gauntlets that improve the overall equipped build; do not pay a metal premium expecting stronger punches.

Affixes, rarity and sockets

The rarity ladder is Common, Magic, Rare, Legendary, Aetheric and Artifact (Gilded is a special boss-loot tier). Magic and Rare items have rolled affixes; Legendary items are named records with fixed powers; Aetheric pieces belong to a set. Artifact items are fixed records. Rarity describes the item’s properties, not a guarantee that every line helps a fist build.

Prioritize:

  • Hand-to-Hand skill, Speed, Strength, Luck, and Agility on wearable gear. Speed and Strength are the measured offensive priorities; Agility improves hit chance, and Luck supports Critical Strike.
  • Damage where a line is compatible with the actual attack. The weapon damage affix and weapon damage gems apply to a weapon item; they do not turn Gauntlets into a fist weapon. Read the card’s comparison against the worn set before keeping it.
  • Armor and resistance help survival. Thorns on armor can return some damage when you are hit. The regular leech affix is weapon-only, so it does not heal a bare-hand build; weapon damage, elemental damage and slayer lines are also weapon-only. Ignore focus, spell potency and cast speed on this no-magic build.
  • The Legendary fist power Open Hand: the bare-handed hit is repeated for half its damage. This is a strong fit when it appears on Gauntlets of the Rose.

Sockets are physical wells. Armour and jewellery hold one each; weapons can hold one to three based on hand size. The original socket pass gives a socket to Rare items at 15% and Legendary items at 30%. A weapon with no socket gets one in its later pass at 12% Magic / 25% Rare / 40% Legendary; further Rare and Legendary sockets roll at 30% / 45% each, stopping on a miss. Socketing is free and can be done from the pack, including on worn gear; Reforge extracts a gem whole for 50 / 100 / 200 / 400 / 800 gold by grade. A gem type contributes no more than one Rare-band line on a piece. On armour, Gauntlets and jewellery, gems do not add fist damage, Strength, Speed or Hand-to-Hand; those offensive gem lines are only on a held weapon. Instead, look for Amber for Luck (up to +6), Malachite for Endurance (up to +8), or a resistance gem: Ruby (fire), Sapphire (frost), Emerald (poison) or Diamond (magic), up to 20% resistance at Perfect grade. A weapon-only damage or leech gem is for a weapon set, not bare hands.

Sigil sets, Aetheric sets and Legendaries

The best offensive set theme is Dagon's Brand: its 2-piece tier adds 2-6 Strength and 4-12 Critical Strike; later tiers say 4-12% more weapon damage (doubled below half health) and 4-10% weapon damage per kill, up to 3 stacks for 12 seconds. The stat tier suits fists. The latter tiers are worded for weapons, so do not count them as fist bonuses. For survival, Malacath's Bulwark gives 2-5 armor per part and 2-6 Endurance at 2 pieces; at 6, a fatal hit leaves 1 health and halves damage for 4-8 seconds, recovering in 300-150 seconds. Aetheric Ruhn's Regalia adds 15-45 fire resistance and 2-6 fire damage per weapon hit at 2 pieces, then a 25-60% melee cleave to a second foe within 3 m; its 6-piece Nova deals 10-40 damage under 25% health and grants 10-25% more weapon damage for 10 seconds, recovering in 180-90 seconds. Regalia's fire resistance is useful to Tiger Fist; its weapon-only blow bonuses should not be assumed to affect fists. It is Daedric and allowed by Tiger Fist's forbidden-metal list, but is end-game gate loot. Sets wake online when the character's Renown is known, and sleep in duels.

Useful Legendary records include Gauntlets of the Rose for Open Hand and any armour or jewellery whose fixed power heals, reduces damage, or strengthens melee hits. Legendaries are fixed records: check the named effect rather than judging by tier or item name. Quests that offer a reward card show a weapon, armour and jewel option tailored to your armour and best weapon skill; compare the card’s stats, material and effects. Take armour or jewellery if no compatible fist Gauntlet is offered. The Play Guide explains quest reward tiers and reward cards.

Tempering and Reforge

Tempering is a Smithing job for weapons, plate, shields and chain, or an Outfitting job for leather and cloth. It raises a found Rare-or-lower piece one quality step, up to Superior; it cannot temper Masterwork, already-Superior, Legendary, Aetheric, Artifact, Gilded or Sigil pieces. It costs half the recipe’s main material input, rounded up, and the craft XP at the item’s recipe tier. A typical weapon or armour is a useful target if that step improves condition or weight; it does not reroll its affixes.

There are two Reforge actions in the current systems. Loot Reforge rerolls one affix on an identified, unequipped Magic or Rare piece. It costs 2 Welkynd Shards + 100 gold for Magic or 4 shards + 400 gold for Rare. An Exalted Legendary can reroll its extra line for 10 shards + 2,000 gold. It awards no XP; only the original selected line can be rolled again. Profession Reforge is the Enchanting-50 craft service: it rerolls one property on a Magic or Rare piece for 2 or 5 Arcane Essence, respectively, with no craft XP. The hone raises a line within its current band; first hone costs 1 shard + 50 gold for Magic, 2 + 150 for Rare, or 4 + 500 for Exalted Legendary, then the price doubles for each hone already taken on that piece. The selected Reforge action and the item card state what it will accept. Legendary, Aetheric, Artifact, Gilded and Sigil pieces cannot be tempered or ordinarily reforged. Socket setting is free; Reforge extraction returns the gem whole at the grade prices above. Treat all of this as a late investment: first confirm the item is wearable and legal, and its lines apply to your fists. A fist has no weapon condition to repair, but worn armour can still benefit from maintenance. Crafted gear tops out at Masterwork; pursue higher-tier drops and set pieces for end-game power.

7. Consumables and survival

With no useful magicka, carry brewed consumables rather than planning around spells. Potions can provide healing, fatigue recovery, poison and disease cures, levitation, and water breathing. Confirm the exact effect and duration on each potion card; recipes and potion strength determine what a batch actually makes. Cure Poison is especially helpful with Willpower 15. Carry levitation and water-breathing potions for routes that otherwise assume magic.

Climates & Calories adds food, water, temperature and fatigue pressures when its survival feature is enabled. It has Off, Boons and Hard modes; Boons is the default. Food banks against hunger, a cooked and fresh meal feeds better than raw or stale food, and spoiled or raw items risk illness depending on the mode. The survival feed, not generic inventory food text, shows current need. Carry rations and a full waterskin, and use cooked provisions before a long trip. The guide’s cooking recommendation remains Provisioning.

At Alchemy 50, Brewer makes three potions per brew; the alternative Distiller adds 10 percentage points to the chance of a Potent result. A Potent potion gets +25% magnitude or duration where that potion supports potency. At Alchemy 100, Master Alchemist raises that bonus to +40%; the alternative Transmuter supports material transmutation. These choices are useful for a no-magic fighter, but the craft limit means choose the two crafts you will take above Journeyman with care (The Play Guide). Brew cures and travel utility first, then healing and fatigue support.

Points: Renown stars and profession Craft points

Your Renown level gives 2 Stars for each level after Renown 1. The formula is 2 × (Renown − 1): Renown 5 gives 8 Stars; Renown 10 gives 18; Renown 50 gives 98. Stars can buy combat or life stars. Combat stars must be paid from the Renown-Star purse.

At 100 ranks in all ten tracks, that is 125 Craft points. Craft points can buy life-ring stars only. They cannot buy combat stars. When paying for life stars, the game uses Craft points first, then any remaining Renown Stars.

For this guide's future profession scenario, 100 ranks each in Herbalism, Mining, Smithing and Provisioning total 400 ranks, or 50 Craft points. This is a planning assumption, not a claim about Tiger Fist's current ranks.

How to allocate without getting lost

  1. Choose a start. For Tiger Fist, the plan below starts at The Steed.
  2. Buy a star connected to the start or to a star you already own. A path preview shows the shortest route to a distant star. If you select that destination, you must pay for every unowned star on the route.
  3. Continue outward along stars you have already bought. You cannot jump to an isolated star or pass through another sign's start. A keystone ends a route.
  4. Watch the two purses. Combat stars cannot cost more than your Renown Stars. All stars together cannot cost more than Stars plus Craft points; Craft points can only cover life-ring stars.

pathTo finds the shortest route from the allocated set. allocatable is true when a star is one step away. The service checks the saved tree with treeFault: the start must exist, every key must be a real and unique star, every allocated star must connect back to the start, and the total must fit the purses. fits checks the purse totals. If you refund a connecting star, the game also refunds stars that would no longer connect.

Why stacking one damage bonus stops helping

The Firmament first caps each individual stat channel. For example, one weapon family damage channel can add at most 30%, a combat skill at most +15, Strength at most +15, and evasion at most a 10% chance. The channel caps are listed in STAT_DEFS.

Then DFO caps combinations when a blow is calculated. For a fist blow, the general weapon, melee and fist channels all contribute. Their ordinary combined gain is capped at +30% (and has a −20% floor). Extra conditions such as an unaware foe or a wounded attacker are added after that, but the whole blow bonus is capped at +50%. That is why another melee-damage star can stop adding damage once the shared cap is full, even if that star's own channel has not reached its individual cap. A keystone's drawback still counts after the positive gain cap.

Spell-cost reduction has its own 25% group cap. Tiger Fist does not use spells, so this is not part of the plan. The life ring has different channels, such as profession XP and yield, carry weight, travel fatigue and gathering-action bonuses; those do not feed into combat.

Where your companion came from

A revenant is a particular enemy the game remembers. A sworn revenant is one you defeated and chose to Spare. It is not an undead class you bought or a random companion assigned at login. I cannot reconstruct your particular companion's past without its record. The Revenants page shows remembered deeds.

The current life cycle is:

  1. A special enemy carries the Unquiet mark. One in four eligible elites or champions is marked. Its title starts with Unquiet. Online, new revenants require Renown 10, which is separate from your character level. The enemy must be at least level 3. Ordinary enemies, allies, the watch, quest foes, summoned foes, another player's puppets, warband followers, Arena fighters and Open Zone foes cannot become your new revenants.
  2. It survives a consequential fight with you. A marked foe can become one by killing you, fleeing successfully, felling a companion, or routing you. Routing has a specific test: it recently hurt you, you fell below half health during that fight, and then you get 70 m away or teleport out. Simply walking through a door is not that test.
  3. Flight is a major source. A marked newcomer gets one 50% flee roll below 35% health. It runs for 8 seconds, at 2.5 times its walk speed. It escapes beyond 45 m, or when its run ends more than 12 m from you. Otherwise it is cornered and fights on. In shared dungeons this applies to foes owned separately by you, not the room's shared layout foes.
  4. It gets a name and returns. It starts at rank I and normally becomes due after 2–4 days of your character's clock. Brutal, Zealous and Unhinged revenants have the Vengeance ambition: 1–2 days, then 55% of eligible encounter rolls rather than the usual 40%. These are encounter chances after it is due, not a guaranteed appointment. At most three hunt you; no new one is born while that limit is full. The Dread is counted separately.
  5. Surviving raises its rank, up to V. A successful ordinary escape, slipping away from your judgement, or routing you raises an existing revenant each time. A killing-blow escape caused by unbroken will raises it only every second time. Killing you or felling a companion also raises it only every second time. A rank increase clears both partial counts. Your death is not required to develop a rival.
  6. Beat it, then choose Spare. The final blow leaves your own revenant kneeling at 1 health. Activate it to open Kill / Spare, select Spare, and confirm. It becomes sworn. It joins your side if a companion slot is open; otherwise it waits Away. With six already sworn, you must release one before sparing another. Left kneeling for over 90 seconds of running world time, or left more than 60 m away, it slips away. The judgement window itself holds that wait.

"Unquiet" means eligible to become a remembered rival. Rank I–V measures that rival's history and strength. Neither is the companion's separate level 1–20 used for stars.

A hostile revenant that kills you online can also take one eligible item at respawn: your equipped weapon or a draw from the pack's five most valuable eligible pieces. It keeps at most three stolen items. Gold, letters of credit, locked pieces, quest items, summoned items, the Materials Bag, horses, carts and deeds are among the refusals. Binding alone is not an exclusion in this theft function; locking is. Sparing it returns what it took; executing it leaves the items in its pile. This theft is a hostile rival's rule, not something a loyal companion does when you fall.

Odds, rewards and risks

Success odds = clamp(55% + 10 percentage points × (rank + gear power + Road odds strength − danger) + Devoted bonus, 30%, 95%).

Each usable given gear piece adds 0.05 power, plus 0 / 0.10 / 0.20 / 0.35 for Common / Magic / Rare / Legendary. Total gear power caps at 2, worth at most 20 percentage points. There is no errand death, permanent loss or capture outcome in these modules. There is an injured/resting outcome. At most two can be abroad. A recovering one cannot start; an abroad one cannot be called, released, equipped or have its stars changed. Its Road modifiers are copied when it sets out. The actual reward formulas are:

  • Coin: floor(your character level × province days × a seeded value from 10 up to 18 × [1 + 0.25 × (danger − 1)] × [1 + 0.10 × (rank − 1)] × venture gold multiplier × result share × [1 + haul stars]). This uses your level at return, not companion level. For Tiger Fist at level 7, a successful bare rank-I High Rock Sell-sword gives 70–125 coin, before any later level change.
  • Materials: base units are (3 + 2 × danger) × province days × venture materials multiplier × result share, then haul stars increase them, with integer flooring. Draws favour cheaper goods by inverse material tier. Tier 5+ goods are limited to one per day of the actual road; later draws use cheaper goods where available.
  • Gear: Plunder counts actual road days after Road time modifiers. A fractional road day gets a seeded chance of one extra day for gear and the fine-material allowance. Shorter missions therefore do not retain all the long trip's guaranteed pieces. Forage and Sell-sword keep their per-errand piece chance. Haul stars add no pieces.
  • Rarity per piece: Legendary 0.5% + 0.4% × danger + 0.3% × (rank − 1); Rare 6% + 3% × danger + 2% × (rank − 1); Magic 50%; otherwise Common. These are separate portions of one roll. The maximum Legendary chance is 3.7%, at rank V / danger V. Pieces are minted at your character level at return.
  • Companion XP: the rule in the levels section, paid on both successful and hard roads only while below level 10 at return. No direct character XP, Renown or Marks reward appears in the errand haul or return code.

Coin goes to your purse, gear to your inventory, and materials to the Materials Bag then inventory as needed. A return is processed on the companion layer's next running frame, not an interactive battle abroad. The errand is held in your character save; I found no separate account-service mission scheduler. The account service does enforce Open Zone companion inventory drops.

Why the missions feel shallow

They currently are choose a destination and reward emphasis, wait, then receive a seeded result. There is no mission map to play, tactical party assignment, sequence of encounters, dialogue branch, rescue from capture or province-specific story in this implementation. Province changes duration, danger, material pool and city text. Venture changes the reward mix. Rank, given gear, Devoted loyalty and Road stars modify the calculation.

FEUD's memory, adaptations, weakness, will, last stands, bands, lairs, theft, loyalty, desertion and betrayal are built. The later Unquiet trails, training, wounds and Dread are also built. Attacking

Check the swing mode. The online defaults set Click or Hold. In Controls, WeaponSwingMode is 2: either a click or holding attack starts swings. Each click chooses a random direction from six: up-right, left, right, down-left, down, or down-right. If you choose Gesture, hold attack and drag; the drag direction chooses the swing. Gesture is the classic default, but it is not DFO Online's shipped default. (WeaponAttackThreshold controls how far you must drag.)

The click mode does not pick straight up. For damage, that means down is the hardest swing but also the least accurate; left or right has no tradeoff. These are flat formula modifiers, not percentage changes.

A fist swing reaches 2.5 m (2.25 m plus a 0.25 m cast radius), and still needs the foe in view with clear line of sight. A melee swing has five frames; the hit lands on frame two. The time per frame follows live Speed, strength and weapon heft, and weapon handling. Bare hands use the faster 0.9 handling factor. The clock has a 0.09-second minimum and 0.4-second maximum per frame, so there is no single swing rate for every character. Defence: move, do not wait to block

Tiger Fist has no active block or timed parry input. A shield can automatically block some attacks if equipped; its chance and protection are resolved by the combat formula. The Shield Widget only draws and animates the shield in first person. The Weapon Widget is a weapon display and recoil effect, not a parry control. An enemy can also turn a connected hit into zero damage; that is a hit result, not a parry button you can press.

Move out of the marked attack shape. When a telegraphed attack misses, the enemy overreaches and is open to punishment. A perfect dodge is specifically being inside the marked area at the late sample, 0.25 seconds before impact, then outside when it lands. That makes the open window 1.5 times longer. The foe takes 30% more damage while open; your hit that staggers it can hold it longer. Watch the ground and step clear rather than backing straight away from a tracking lunge or charge.

Armour does not make attacks miss more often. Under the shipped Physical Combat and Armor Overhaul, the attack first hits or misses; armour then reduces damage. A shield's automatic block is another part of that damage calculation.

Ordinary landed blows can knock a foe back. The poise and stagger system matters while a foe winds up: each hit adds weight to its poise, and reaching the threshold breaks the wind-up and staggers it. Your fist blow has less poise weight than a blunt weapon, so do not expect one punch to cancel a heavy wind-up. Iron tells cannot be staggered by adding poise; the blow holds them in place, but does not build their meter. The foe's poise bar is not a player poise meter. Enemy attacks can still shove, rattle, or knock you down when their shape lands.

Read the enemy before closing

Enhanced AI tells are on for everyone online. Look for the foe's body glint and ground shape; listen for its wind-up and release cues. A feint cuts its mark, so do not commit to a dodge until the attack is real. The target bar can show Iron, Staggered, or Open. The wind-up and landing times vary by attack, and elites and champions can use a broader set of shapes.

Fists ignore weapon-material requirements because an unarmed attack has no weapon material to check. That makes fist damage reliable against foes whose hides turn away low-metal weapons. It does not protect you from their attacks.

Prioritize ranged threats. Archers and ranged casters can attack before you reach them; the AI treats bows and ranged spells as shooter attacks. Close distance using cover when possible, then keep moving so an aimed shot or spell does not land cleanly. Poison is another serious threat: your build's low Willpower makes poison resistance weak. Carry Cure Poison and resistance potions as advised in The Play Guide.

Elites carry a gold halo. Champions are named foes with a visible trait such as Mighty, Swift, or Vampiric. Inspect the name, halo, and health bar before you commit. The play guide explains when they appear and their added danger. Revenants are marked returning foes that can grow stronger after they kill you or escape at low health. Fatigue and stamina

Your fatigue bar is your combat stamina. A melee swing spends fatigue when it reaches its hit frame, whether it hits or misses. The base cost is 11 before your fatigue modifiers. Running, jumping, climbing, poison, and some other effects also drain it. Haymaker and Flying Kick each cost 4 fatigue; see section 5 of Building Your Character for when those attacks can be used.

Do not let the bar reach zero while enemies are nearby. Online, exhaustion near foes costs 10% of your health each time it triggers and displays a warning; it does not instantly kill you. In safe, dry conditions, exhaustion instead causes a recovery hour. A Restore Fatigue potion is the direct in-fight recovery option. Put one on your hotbar before a dungeon so you can drink it without opening the pack. Poison can drain fatigue too, so treat the poison status promptly.

Early money

Sell dungeon loot

At an NPC counter, the price starts from item value, quality, condition and the counter's quality. Online, Mercantile and Personality both affect your haggle. The game clamps each skill to 0–100; the classic haggle weights Mercantile 179 and Personality 51. Online, the offer uses that haggle result relative to the result at 100 in both skills, with the online sale-skill weight set to 0.5 and the best haggler's half-price ceiling. Banking and keeping money safe

Online banking uses the Empire's single account. The client maps regional bank counters to that Empire account, so your balance can be deposited and withdrawn at any region. That is different from offline regional accounts. Gold in the account is not carried weight. Carried coins weigh 0.0025 kg each; a withdrawal is refused if it would put you over your encumbrance limit.

You can turn account gold into a letter of credit worth its face value. The letter is an inventory item, weighs nothing, and can be deposited later. Its issue costs a 1% commission from the account, and the minimum letter is 100 gold. A letter can pay for eligible transactions, but the value does not become account gold until you deposit it.

Loans exist online, but the Empire lends only a tenth of the ordinary limit. Roleplay & Realism (always on online) sets that limit at 10,000 gold per level by default, so online you can borrow 1,000 gold per level: at level 7, at most 7,000. There is one loan at a time. Ten percent interest is added immediately; the repayment date is one game year later. If overdue, account funds are taken first. Online, if that is not enough, the Empire can draw from the other regional balances; an unpaid remainder becomes a default.

A respawn death takes one tenth of the gold in your purse, rounded down. It does not take bank gold or letters of credit. Keep only the spendable cash you need on a trip and deposit excess. Marks, Soul Cakes and Weir Keys are held by the account service; physical inventory theft does not reach those balances. Letters, Shards and other bound items follow their own item rules.

Can another player hurt you?

In ordinary co-op, other players cannot hit you with melee, arrows, spells or weapon techniques. Duels are the consensual exception: one player challenges, the other accepts, and the bout runs outdoors inside a bounded ring. A duel cannot hurt your character: the relay referees it on a separate duel vitality bar, and your real health is never touched (nobody drops to 1 health and nothing is healed). The duel is not a license for bystanders to attack.

The Arena runs separate refereed bouts and matchmaking in Arena rooms. It is PvP by the match rules, not an open town fight. Sieges are separate, refereed battle rooms for competing sides; player attacks are judged by the siege referee. The Wrothgarian Mountains Open Zone is the non-consensual exception: PvP is enabled in its wilderness and its dungeons. See The Play Guide for its danger and death-drop warning.

Dungeon loot in an ordinary shared room is first-come: another player may take loose loot before you do. There is no general “loot stealing” crime for ordinary dungeon drops. Shared bodies and containers use their own take rules; a stranger taking goods from a protected shelf, cupboard or shop is a crime. See The Play Guide for how public and private rooms work.

Crime and the law

Theft includes taking a protected person's goods; shop shelves and strangers' cupboards are protected targets. Trespass and breaking and entering are crimes, as are assault and murder. A crime can be charged when the watch catches you; getting away or dying ends the active chase but does not repay the standing loss. The law tracks standing by region.

Guards can stop a known criminal they see in town. At legal standing below -10, the watch can demand a fine or arrest; refusing starts the arrest chase. A paid fine improves standing, and a court sentence repays the charge for lesser crimes. Assault, Murder and Treason leave half their legal standing loss as a lasting mark even after sentence. An acquittal restores the full charge. A Temple pardon can lift banishment; Murder or Treason can lead to a 30-day banishment. To settle an active crime, answer the watch or court; dying or getting away only ends the chase. A bounty contract is separate: completing one on the regional board improves that region's legal standing by two points.

A Curfew is an edict chosen by a town's ruling seat. At night its guards are stronger, and crimes there cost twice the legal reputation. It is a rule of that town, not a world-wide curfew. See City Control and Guilds for how seats and edicts change.

Disease, vampirism and lycanthropy

Ordinary diseases come from disease-bearing attacks and effects; a successful infection applies the disease's own stat and fatigue effects over time. Tiger Fist's class has Immunity to Disease, so ordinary disease infection is blocked. Special infections for vampirism and lycanthropy bypass ordinary disease immunity; being disease-immune does not protect you from those curses.

Vampirism and lycanthropy are separate infections with quest and cure paths. Vampirism grants its clan spells and disease immunity, but its stats are reduced by 20 when the vampire is bareheaded in sunlight; a raised hood protects the stats. It no longer burns the vampire in sunlight. A lycanthrope gains its +30 to listed skills, including Hand-to-Hand, only in beast form; its human form keeps the character's ordinary combat values. It must kill an innocent about once per character month or suffer the curse's health loss. Transformation and that recurring requirement are costs; the curse is not a free permanent fist bonus.

For Tiger Fist, lycanthropy has the more direct fist tie: its beast-form +30 includes Hand-to-Hand, and BAL6 lets the claws use the bare-hand combat route. Those benefits are beast-only, with a monthly innocent-kill requirement and forced transformation to manage. Vampirism's disease immunity is redundant for this class, and its clan spells do not fit the no-magic build; its stat benefit depends on time of day and the hood. Either curse is a separate playstyle choice, not a free permanent bonus.

The “cart method” is a hired carriage

Yes, land travel with a cart-like vehicle exists. The carriage is a paid fast-travel service, separate from the horse cart or wagon you buy and drive yourself.

Find the carriage driver standing with a carriage and horses just outside a walled city's gate. Only cities have one: the driver stands in four specific city-gate blocks (WALLAA08–WALLAA11), and on the player's own map data 410 of the Bay's 3,444 settlements have such a gate, all of them cities. Hamlets and villages never do. The nearest to Privateer's Hold is Chesterwark, about 11 map squares (~9 km) south and slightly west; Daggerfall city also has one. The guide's map can filter towns by carriage. Talk to the driver and choose Fast Travel. The driver's map lets you choose an eligible town, village, or other allowed destination. Confirm the displayed fare and the game performs Daggerfall-style fast travel. You board at the city gate, not at the Stable or Wagon Yard. Immersive Travel's carriage records are added to the gate blocks; the driver is not a merchant-yard transport seller.

The fee has two parts: a daily carriage fee for each whole land-travel day, plus one more daily fee; and, if you selected inn stops, inn charges. The current setting defaults to 1 gold per day. So with inns off and default settings, the carriage fee is one gold plus one gold for each complete land-travel day. The fare screen gives the exact amount. Long trips can also include a ship fare if ship travel is selected and enabled.

Online, this driver service is the way to take the paid, instant land trip. The held map's own land journeys are walked. A carriage is still subject to location eligibility and, when the regional setting is enabled, its region restrictions. In the Open Zone, fast travel cannot enter or leave the zone. Inside, the applicable Open Zone fee and cooldown also apply.

Walking and the Overworld Travel View

Walking is free and gives you the most control. You can travel at ground level, or open the Overworld Travel View and click a town, quest mark, or map point to walk there. The route planner scores road steps at 1, tracks at 1.6, and open ground at 3.5, so it can favor a longer road over a cross-country shortcut.

The Travel View has travel-speed settings of x5, x10, x20, x30, x40, x60 and x100. The Portal Stone

The Portal Stone replaces a land teleport for a character who cannot use magic. Buy one from a shopkeeper's service row for 5 Welkynd Shards. The shards are the currency from dismantling qualifying gear. The stone is bound and consumed one use at a time.

Use it outdoors under open sky, choose a place on the map, then walk through the portal that opens in front of you. The portal remains for 30 seconds, and other nearby players can use it too. Closing the map without choosing a destination spends nothing. The arrival is immediate and has no travel fare or travel cooldown.

Horses

Buy a horse at a town's Stable. A Stable offers three individually generated horses for that town's day; the listed price varies with its stats. A horse's Speed stat changes its ride pace, and holding sprint asks it to gallop. Galloping uses stamina; a winded horse must recover before galloping again.

A Stable lets you change its coat, pattern, and leg markings for free; these are visual choices.

Your own cart or wagon

Buy a Small Cart, Open Wagon, or Caravan at a town's Wagon Yard. Owning one means its item is in your pack; the Horse Cart and Cargo system can deploy it behind you and haul your carried goods. Ships

There are two ways to travel by ship.

Buy passage on a friendly ship. At a port, find a friendly ship moored at its berth whose hull can carry a passenger: a Large Boat, Small Ship, or Carrack. Activate the ship and choose a port you have discovered. The displayed fare is 25 gold per 24 map pixels, plus 25 gold, counting the entire voyage as sea. You pay even if you own a ship. The ship actually sails the Overworld crossing; it does not teleport. You must stay aboard for the passage to continue. Stepping ashore or otherwise leaving ends the passage, and goods in the ship's hold come ashore with you. A hostile encounter can interrupt the voyage; while you remain aboard, the captain resumes sailing.

Own a ship. Shipyards stand at ports. Buy hull parts or a deed there, assemble and place the boat, and sail it yourself; hire named crew at the port's Crew Hall if you need hands. Hull base prices are Rowboat 4,000, Large Boat 8,000, Small Ship 100,000, Large Galley 200,000, and Carrack 150,000 gold. Repairs, crew and supplies cost extra. The Shipyard sells every hull; crew fees are shown for each recruit. An owned vessel gives you physical sea travel and is required to cross the Veil into the Eltheric Ocean. Passage-on-a-friendly-ship is only offered within the Bay.

Risks and range. Hostile ships and sea events can interrupt a voyage. In the Bay, the sea has pirate traffic and the scheduled sea-serpent event (What's New Since 17 October). Beyond the Veil, the Eltheric Ocean's deeper bands have more dangerous corsairs and legendary ships. The Veil crossing is a sailed transition, not a Portal Stone, Recall, carriage, or passage route. Use the Fleet/Summon feature to reunite with a ship left on the far side.

The Open Zone: travel rules and risk

The Wrothgarian Mountains are an Open Zone. Ordinary fast travel cannot cross its boundary: you cannot teleport or take a fast trip into or out of the zone. Use the Overworld and its roads to enter, leave, and travel between its heights. Inside the zone, fast travel costs 2,500, 5,000, 10,000, or 15,000 gold, based on the ring where you arrive, and has a 10-minute cooldown. This applies to carriage travel inside as well. Overworld walking does not pay that fee or wait for that cooldown. Its travel speed is capped at x20 in wild ground and x40 on a road.

This is dangerous PvP country. See The Play Guide before going; in particular, a death can cost the wagon and bag you brought in. Do not treat the fast-travel fee as protection from the zone's other risks.

How Tiger Fist should travel

At level 7, with limited play time and no magic, use the cheapest method that fits the destination:

City Control and Guilds

For DFO Online, studied against main commit 4bba28c6c (2026-10-20). This describes the built rules, not a live reading of who holds what today. src/ paths below are inside the upstream source tree.

A town’s seat is its player-guild Charter. One guild holds it at a time; that guild can rule parts of the town’s economy and build defenses. Other guilds compete by pledging influence, then may earn a scheduled siege. You can follow the contest without joining a large guild, and you can help materially even if your character is not ready to lead a war.

Which places have seats?

The three Crown seats are Daggerfall, Wayrest and Sentinel. Each is a major city and its castle is the seat-holding guild’s hall. A Palace seat is every other base-game location whose building records contain a Palace. A hub without a Palace is not a seat. The code derives the list from the world’s locations at startup, so the complete list can depend on the installed base data; the Overworld map and each town’s card are the practical way to find them. A location is only one seat even if its records contain several Palaces.

The three Crowns cover their kingdoms; other seats lie in the kingdom regions, the three contested Marches (Betony, Anticlere and Lainlyn), or the Free Lands. A crown-holder gains influence reach in its own kingdom and the relevant Marches. Free Lands have no crown reach. A town with no Palace has no seat Charter, even if it is a regional hub.

Influence and the weekly Turning

Guilds choose where their work counts. Each week an Officer or Guildmaster can pledge to one seat in a region, across up to five regions. A guild holding a seat is automatically pledged there and cannot pledge to another seat in that region. Work done in an unpledged region earns no seat influence. Pledges lock for the final 48 hours, the Reckoning.

The week is real time: Sunday 18:00 UTC begins the Muster; Friday 18:00 UTC begins the Reckoning; the Turning settles the week on the next Sunday at 18:00. The service does not need a scheduled job: the first seat request after the boundary settles that week once. During the Reckoning you can still add influence, but pledges are fixed and standings are visible.

What holding a seat gives

The Charter and its upkeep. The guild pays 2,500 Marks a week for a Palace or 15,000 Marks for a Crown, multiplied by the server’s activity scale from 0.4 to 1.5. Holding more than one seat increases upkeep by 25% per extra seat-weight and cuts defence by 5% per extra seat-weight; a Palace weighs 1 and a Crown 3, with the heaviest seat excluded from the extra. A short Marks balance puts the Charter into Neglect: -10 Standing for the first short week; missing upkeep a second week in a row lapses the Charter. Standing starts at 50, is capped at 100, and affects defence: each point above 50 adds 0.5%; each point below removes 1%. At zero Standing, the town can revolt. If a siege changes hands, the Charter moves to its winner.

Tithe and shop prices. The holder sets a whole-number Tithe once each week: up to 10% at a Palace and 15% at a Crown (a Market Hall can raise either cap by one point per built tier). It comes from the seller’s proceeds and courier fees at boards in that seat’s bailiwick, and goes into the guild’s Marks treasury. The buyer still pays the listed price; the seller’s proceeds also lose the base 5% sales tax. Tithe and the week’s other actions move Standing. The seat’s own Market Day Edict cuts shop prices by 10% for everyone. Members of the holding guild also get 10% off at a Palace or 15% off at a Crown; Standing of 80 or more adds 5%. Market Day and seat discounts combine, with other town pricing, at the law’s price floor. Town Prosperity still moves prices and depends on size, market cycles, seat standing and edict, raids, sea lanes and player activity. See the town economy entry in What's New Since 17 October for how Prosperity affects prices and what players can do for it.

Sieges: the scheduled battle

A Right of Siege names an attacker. The holder sets the day and start hour from Wednesday through Saturday, between 16:00 and 02:00 UTC; the default is Wednesday 20:00. Battles are moved into non-overlapping two-hour slots if one of their guilds already has another fight. Crown fights have fixed Saturday slots: Daggerfall at 20:00, Wayrest at 21:00, Sentinel at 22:00 UTC. The client announces battles at the Turning and 24 hours, 1 hour and 5 minutes before they start. The roster closes 10 minutes before battle. A Palace fight runs 30 minutes; a Crown fight 45 minutes.

The signing rules make this a guild fight, not an open arena. A fighter must be a member of the side’s guild for 7 days at the Turning and have been bound to that guild during the week that won the Right. The signing also binds that account to a side for the fight. A Guildmaster may hire up to 2 Palace or 4 Crown Sellswords; each non-member can serve only one side that week, and cannot have fought for the opposing guild in the previous four weeks. Anyone else may spectate; a room admits up to 60 spectators. Palace sides are capped at 10, Crown sides at 20. This referee normalizes fighter health and clips hits and movement; battle results do not take your save’s gear or gold.

At a Palace, attackers capture the Gate, Market and Temple banners, then hold the Throne. Take 2 of 3 banners to open the Throne and hold it uncontested for 120 seconds. A Palace with a tier-3 Walls project also has a Gatehouse that must be breached. At a Crown, attackers must take 3 of 4 banners and breach the Gatehouse before holding the Throne for 180 seconds. A Gatehouse starts at 20,000 vitality; attackers’ melee blows do one tenth damage to it. A Ram deals 500 damage every 10 seconds while 2 attackers stand within 3 metres. A Ram has 3,000 vitality. Defenders respawn every 20 seconds at a Palace or 30 seconds at a Crown; Walls shorten that wave interval by 3 seconds per tier, to a 5-second minimum. If time runs out, the holder keeps the seat. At an unheld Contested seat, the two claimants play a 20-minute Tourney over the banners without a Throne. The side holding more banners wins; a tie goes to higher influence, and the winner still has to pay the Charter fee.

The holder wins more than time: if attackers raised a banner, a successful defense adds 15 Standing and 20% defence at the next Turning. A win for the attacker takes the Charter; each fortification falls one tier and the former holder loses its Legacy there. An attacker who does not show forfeits after ten minutes; the holder gets 10 Standing and the same next-defense bonus. Fighters on either side can earn a once-per-Season Honour: each eligible fighter gets 50 Marks and 2,000 Renown XP for the winning side, 25 Marks and 1,000 XP for the losing side, plus a Warforged Steel, Standard-bearer’s Silk or Siege-cracked Gem roll. These rewards are separate from the Charter outcome.

Walls, Rams and other works

The holder builds from its stockpile and pays the upgrade’s Marks cost. A project stands 2, 4 or 7 days after its last needed material arrives. Masonry’s Builder specialization at 50 uses 10% less stone; a Masonry 100 Fortifier on the defending roster can save the Walls from dropping at a capture once per Season. Carpentry 100’s Siegewright makes its project stand a day sooner and adds 50% Ram vitality. Writs posted for a seat can request the materials its stockpile needs.

Walls quicken defender waves. A Gatehouse raises gate vitality by 50% per tier (up to tier 3); it can be built at a Crown or at a Palace whose Walls are tier 3. Watchtowers warn the holder when the strongest challenger crosses half, then a quarter of its defense. Barracks add 2 guards a tier, up to 6. A Market Hall adds 25% listings per tier and one percentage point to the Tithe cap. A Shrine adds 50 gate-kill influence per tier (up to 2) and +1 Standing per tier. Forge, Workshop and Apothecary are described above. A Harbour can be built only at a coastal seat.

The attacker’s Siege Camp uses the same material writs. At the Turning, camp stock is spent; Ram Kits can become Rams for that battle. Delivering self-gathered materials is useful even if you will not fight: each eligible Mark of value adds one influence to the guild’s contest and supplies either its camp or its fortifications.

Player guilds, Marks and chapters

You can join a player guild from an invitation, or found one if you have 10,000 gold and Renown 10. A guild has up to 50 characters. Its four ranks are Guildmaster, Officer, Member and Recruit; the Guildmaster may rename those ranks. Officers can invite, remove and promote or demote lower ranks, pledge seats, and operate held-seat levers. The Guildmaster alone withdraws from the guild’s gold treasury, pays Tribute from the guild’s Marks treasury, hires Sellswords and can relinquish a Charter. Any member can deposit gold or silver, put materials into Stores, and take their own materials back. The Members page spells out what each rank can do.

Guilds have a written ledger and a shared vault: 50 slots, or 100 with a hall; the Guildmaster grants and removes members’ vault access. They can buy a home as a hall, furnish it and display chosen heraldry (field, border and device); a held Palace automatically serves as the hall too. Guild names cost 25,000 gold to change, with a two-week cooldown. A guild’s own Mark balance and its gold treasury are separate: Marks are account-server currency, capped at 10,000,000 in a guild balance; gold pays gold fees such as founding or renaming a guild, while seat claims, upkeep, works, Edicts and Tribute use Marks. Marks cannot be bought with gold. The Empire’s Bank exchanges Marks for gold at 8 gold per Mark, up to 300 per account per UTC day. Tribute burns guild Marks for influence.

A solo player contributes most reliably by joining a guild, then doing the work that fits a short session: patrol a pledged seat town while moving to earn Watch ticks; complete a gate or a town defense in a region the guild has pledged; or gather, craft and deliver requested materials to the board’s Seat writ. Keep the delivered units your own: bought materials earn far less. Guild influence only counts after your character has been in the guild seven days. A home in the target town helps its guild, if you already own one. A small guild can focus one seat, make sure its accounts spread out across eligible sources, and time a pledge before Friday’s lock. A few members will not casually reach the 6,000 Palace claim threshold alone, but each person can add measurable influence and help secure a siege right or defend a Charter.

There is also a separate Chapters system: your standing and ranks in the classic NPC Fighters, Mages and other guild factions. Player guilds can also maintain regional chapters, with their own Merit, hall writs, seasonal events and a separate ladder for NPC-guild offices. Those are not the town Charters described here. Joining an NPC guild and gaining its ranks can provide other benefits, but it does not pledge your player guild to a town seat.

A seat Season lasts 8 weeks. At its end, Standing moves halfway back toward 50, Legacy clears, and each fortification drops one tier, but Charters remain. A Crown holder earns a seasonal Crowned title; a guild that held a seat for the whole Season earns the Keeper title and its members wear that guild’s banner ribbon in the next Season.

How Tiger Fist gets involved

At level 7, start with an existing guild rather than spend time founding one. Join a group whose members are active in the region you visit; ask which seat they are pledged to and whether they need a Watch presence, a writ delivery or a raid defender. A raid defense is a particularly good fit for Renown, combat Marks, the town economy and your NPC-guild chapter standing; an ordinary raid receipt does not itself add player-seat influence. The existing guide explains the Bay’s Watch and town-defense loop. While you are in a pledged seat town, the Watch can separately add influence. If combat isn't convenient, gather materials yourself and deliver them to an active seat writ. The best near-term payoffs are Marks and Renown from legitimate fights, the guild’s eventual siege Honour, and—if your guild holds the Charter—member shop discounts, hall stations and travel services. The Festival’s +5 attributes is also a temporary town benefit.

Catch-up levers

  • Mentor mode is automatic in a party. A higher-level character mentors when the target would be at least three levels below their real level. The group reference is the harmonic mean of partner levels; the target is rounded reference level plus one, capped at the mentor's real level. The mentor's real save is untouched. Skills above 30 scale by sqrt(target / real) and cap at 5 × target + 40; attributes shed a five-point-per-level pool, apportioned above 40; health/damage and gear protection are scaled; the dungeon spawn level and loot tables use the mentored level. The source explicitly says skill tallies above 100 are weighed against real skill, so weak foes do not train the mentor. It does not specify a bonus XP multiplier for the lower-level player.
  • Party Renown is additive, not split. Every party member receives the full kill award, with +10% per additional member in the room; a full party of eight reaches +70%. This improves shared kill efficiency, but does not create catch-up levels for the lower member beyond the same kill award.
  • Legacy heirs inherit a little. Each attribute gets clamp(round((parent − 50)/10), 0, 3); each parent primary/major skill gets clamp(floor(parent skill/20), 0, 3). Heirs also receive one quarter of carried gold up to 9,900 as a credit. This reduces some rebuild cost, but is a family system, not a rested bonus.
  • No rested or returning-player XP bonus was found in the online Renown and mentor laws reviewed for this brief.

Sources: The Star Tree (Firmament)

instance

Your instance is a copy for you and your party only. It is also the dungeon at its full size: online, most public dungeons are built smaller.

Sources: The Play Guide

spawned dungeon

Online only, every empty map square (about 820 m across) has a 30% chance of a spawned dungeon and 10% of an Elite one. They never stand on a road or within 300 m of a town.

Sources: The Play Guide

elite

Elites carry a gold halo.

Sources: Fighting Well

champion

Champions are named foes with a visible trait such as Mighty, Swift, or Vampiric. Inspect the name, halo, and health bar before you commit.

Sources: Fighting Well

revenant

Revenants are marked returning foes that can grow stronger after they kill you or escape at low health. See Companions for revenant details and how a sworn revenant works.

Sources: Fighting Well

Unquiet

A special enemy carries the Unquiet mark. One in four eligible elites or champions is marked.

Sources: Companions

Open Zone

Most of that region is the Open Zone: open PvP, foes 4x stronger, and dying there drops everything in your bags (and your cart), and a player who kills you takes one equipped piece. The loot is double, but it's a place for a geared group, not a new character.

Sources: The Play Guide

seat

A town’s seat is its player-guild Charter. One guild holds it at a time; that guild can rule parts of the town’s economy and build defenses.

Sources: City Control and Guilds

Turning

The week is real time: Sunday 18:00 UTC begins the Muster; Friday 18:00 UTC begins the Reckoning; the Turning settles the week on the next Sunday at 18:00.

Sources: City Control and Guilds

Edict

The Guildmaster or an Officer sets the Edict for the coming week; it takes effect only if the Turning can pay its cost. The same Edict cannot run two weeks in a row, except Market Day.

Sources: City Control and Guilds

march

The three Crowns cover their kingdoms; other seats lie in the kingdom regions, the three contested Marches (Betony, Anticlere and Lainlyn), or the Free Lands. A crown-holder gains influence reach in its own kingdom and the relevant Marches.

Sources: City Control and Guilds

signature ore

Orsinium Area and the Wrothgarian Mountains share Orichalcum as their signature ore.

Sources: The World, Region by Region

Craft points

Craft points come from profession ranks. The law sums your ranks across the ten profession tracks, limits each track to 100, then gives 1 Craft point per 8 total ranks, rounding down.

Sources: The Star Tree (Firmament)

mastery

At 100, the game offers a permanent mastery choice if that career group has room. Tiger Fist can master two of its three primary skills, two of its three major skills, and one of its six minor skills.

Sources: Building Your Character

martial path

A martial path needs two mastered weapon skills from Long Blade, Short Blade, Axe, Blunt Weapon, Archery and Hand-to-Hand. There are fifteen pairs.

Sources: Building Your Character

technique

Techniques are DFO's special moves, one key per weapon. Bare hands have two: Haymaker and Flying Kick.

Sources: The Play Guide

Welkynd Shards

The shards are the currency from dismantling qualifying gear.

Sources: Getting Around

Portal Stone

The Portal Stone replaces a land teleport for a character who cannot use magic. Buy one from a shopkeeper's service row for 5 Welkynd Shards.

Sources: Getting Around

mentor mode

Mentor mode is automatic. In a party, anyone three or more levels above the group is scaled down to it, so a veteran friend fights at your level and the dungeon spawns for it.

Sources: The Play Guide
